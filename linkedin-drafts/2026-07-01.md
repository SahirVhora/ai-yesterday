# LinkedIn Post Draft - 2026-07-01

Everyone is building AI agents. Almost nobody is teaching them when to shut up.

MIT Technology Review covered new research this week on AI agents that assess their own confidence before making decisions in enterprise workflows. The idea: agents that know when they are out of their depth and hand off to a human instead of guessing.

This is the part most agent builders skip.

When I build tools for SAP SuccessFactors consultants -- MCP servers that expose system metadata, scanners that flag configuration debt, migration validators that catch broken references before go-live -- the goal is never to replace judgment. It is to put better information in front of the person who has it.

A permissions scanner should not decide who gets access. It should surface the 12 roles with conflicts and the 3 that look suspicious, then get out of the way.

A cutover tool should not pick the release date. It should flag the 4 dependencies that will break if you pick the wrong one.

Confidence boundaries are a feature, not a limitation. In enterprise systems where the blast radius of a wrong answer is real -- payroll config, role-based permissions, migration mappings -- a tool that says "I'm not sure, here is what I found" is more valuable than one that confidently gives you the wrong answer.

The best enterprise AI will not be the model that never needs a human. It will be the tool that knows exactly when to ask for one.
